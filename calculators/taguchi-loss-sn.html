Taguchi Loss Function and S/N Ratio

Put a cost on being off target and variable: quadratic loss per part, and signal-to-noise ratio to compare settings.

Loss per part L = k(y − T)², with k = A ÷ Δ². Average loss = k(σ² + (mean − T)²): both spread and offset cost money, even inside tolerance. S/N (nominal): 10·log10(mean² ÷ s²); smaller-is-better: −10·log10(mean of y²); larger-is-better: −10·log10(mean of 1/y²). Higher S/N is better; use it to pick settings that are robust to noise. Example: a bore of 10.00 mm ± 0.10 where scrapping a part costs 25.
